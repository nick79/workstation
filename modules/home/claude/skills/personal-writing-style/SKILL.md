# Personal writing style

## Set priorities

- Follow the user's explicit request and the nearest project, publication, or document style guide first. Use this skill only where those instructions leave room.
- Match the audience, purpose, medium, and requested level of formality.
- Preserve the intended meaning, facts, names, dates, conditions, caveats, quotations, citations, and link targets. Flag uncertainty instead of inventing support.

## Write with a clear voice

- Lead with the concrete point, problem, decision, or outcome. Skip generic scene-setting and throat-clearing.
- Sound like a calm, capable colleague. Be direct and willing to make a recommendation, but place judgment after the evidence.
- Prefer specific nouns and verbs. Use active voice when the actor matters and passive voice when the action or result matters more.
- Use natural contractions when they fit. Use `I`, `we`, and `you` only when the genre and speaker relationship support them. Never invent personal experience, opinions, or authority for the user.
- Vary cadence because the ideas vary. Use short sentences for results or turns, medium sentences for actions and claims, and longer sentences for causal chains that need their context together.
- Give each paragraph one main claim. Build transitions from the preceding fact instead of using canned connective phrases.
- Use lists for real sets, comparisons, or procedures. Do not force ordinary prose into bullets or make sections artificially symmetrical.

## Remove synthetic prose

- Replace generic language with the actual actor, action, mechanism, evidence, tradeoff, or consequence.
- Remove meta-outlines, canned openings, chatbot residue, empty importance claims, vague authority, decorative questions, hype, fake candor, and generic encouragement.
- Avoid repeated triples, formulaic contrasts, synonym cycling, manufactured punchlines, stacked fragments, excessive asides, and conclusions that merely replay the opening.
- Treat words such as "delve", "leverage", "robust", "seamless", "crucial", "comprehensive", "moreover", and "ultimately" as warning signs, not automatic bans. Rewrite around the concrete fact; do not swap in a fancier synonym.
- End on the last useful result, decision, recommendation, or limitation. Do not append a generic summary, invitation, or optimistic flourish.

## Keep the presentation quiet

- Use sentence case for headings unless another convention applies.
- Prefer plain ASCII punctuation unless the medium, locale, quoted material, or project guide requires otherwise. Avoid em dashes, en dashes, curly quotes, ellipses, decorative symbols, emoji, and non-breaking spaces.
- Keep formatting proportional to the material. Avoid excessive headings, bold text, one-line paragraphs, and exclamation marks.
- Preserve exact punctuation and spelling in code, commands, identifiers, protocol data, quotations, proper names, and link targets.

## Revise deliberately

Run separate passes for accuracy, originality, voice, fidelity, and mechanics. Compare rewrites with the source text so no claim, qualification, or relationship disappears. Read the final prose slowly; rewrite any passage that sounds generic, monotonous, or less precise than the underlying idea.
